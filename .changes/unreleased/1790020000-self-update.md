title: The installer loads the newest version of itself, without losing what you have typed
---

A browser tab that has been open for an hour is running an hour-old installer, and every fix shipped since then is one the person in front of it does not get. The page now checks its own deploy marker — on load, every quarter of an hour while you are looking at it, and whenever you come back to the tab — and, when a newer build is live, loads it.

Nothing you have filled in is lost to that. The wizard writes its answers down as you go — what to install, your nickname and keys, the server's name, ports and the generated rcon and QTV passwords, the step you were on — and reads them back on the way up, so the new version arrives with the form exactly as you left it. That also covers reloads we did not start: a refresh or a closed-and-reopened tab now resumes where you were instead of starting over.

It knows when not to. An install that is running is never interrupted, and a finished one is left alone. Where a reload would cost you something a web page cannot put back — the folder you granted access to, or a `pak1.pak` you picked — it says a newer version is available and lets you choose the moment, rather than taking it. And if a deploy is announced before it is actually being served, the page stops trying after two attempts instead of reloading in a loop.
